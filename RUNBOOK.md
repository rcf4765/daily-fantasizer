# The Daily Fantasizer — weekly runbook

The weekly newspaper for the **Men Fantasizing** ESPN league (league 1196354715, season 2026, 12 teams, PPR, H2H points, lineup QB/2RB/2WR/TE/FLEX/D-ST/K, 14 regular-season weeks, 6 playoff teams). Owner/requester: Reza Jalali (team 1, Chimpanzini Bananini).

**The approved design is the graphics-first layout introduced with the week 5 edition on 2026-10-13** ("less text, more graphics", subtle Detroit Lions accents, no memes, no trade rumors). Every new edition must look exactly like it: same template, same sections, same chart types, same voice. Only the data and words change. The older text-heavy tabloid design (week 4 reference) is archived in `legacy/` and must not be used.

2. **Pick the week.** The edition covers the most recent *completed* week. NFL weeks end after Monday Night Football; ESPN finalizes overnight. If the user doesn't say, use (current matchup period − 1) on a Tuesday-or-later run.

So: `git pull`, then check `data/latest.json` and that `data/weekN_raw.txt` exists.
   - If it's missing, trigger it: `gh api -X POST repos/rcf4765/daily-fantasizer/actions/workflows/fetch.yml/dispatches -f ref=main -f 'inputs[week]=N'`, poll `gh api "repos/rcf4765/daily-fantasizer/actions/runs?per_page=1"` until `completed`, then `git pull`. "not final" means ESPN hasn't finished the week: stop and report.

4. **Compute facts.** `python3 facts.py data/weekN_raw.txt`. Read `data/weekN_facts.md` in full.

6. **Render.** `python3 render.py N` → `daily-fantasizer-weekN.html`. Leftover placeholders must be `[]`.
7. **Check.** One headless screenshot pass (Playwright): 1200px and 400px wide, full page; `document.documentElement.scrollWidth` must equal the viewport width. Look at the hero starter strips, the glance chart axis, boom/bust names, scatter (crests must not sit on the quadrant labels), heat map, trade cards if present. Fix overflowing copy and re-render once.
8. **Fact-check** every number in the copy against `weekN_facts.md` (or `weekN_raw.txt` / earlier weeks' facts for history claims).
9. **Publish to the league website.** `python3 publish_site.py N`. It copies the edition to `docs/weekN.html`, makes it the homepage `docs/index.html` and rebuilds `docs/archive.html`. GitHub Pages serves `docs/` (deployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` on every push that changes `docs/`; Pages source = GitHub Actions since 2026-10-19, after the built-in deploy runner jammed) at https://rcf4765.github.io/daily-fantasizer/ (archive at `archive.html`), so the league's link never changes; nothing is texted or emailed. `publish_site.py` also adds the home-screen app tags (between `<!--app-->` and `<!--/app-->`, right after `<title>`) to every page in `docs/`; the install link for the league is https://rcf4765.github.io/daily-fantasizer/install.html (added 2026-10-19; Apple doesn't allow a link to add the icon by itself, so the page walks iPhone users through Share → Add to Home Screen and gives Android a one-tap Install button). The `<title>` stays "The Daily Fantasizer".
10. **Save.** Commit `data/weekN_copy.json`, `daily-fantasizer-weekN.html` and `docs/` and push to `main` (the push is what updates the website; Pages rebuilds in a minute or two). Mirror `weekN_raw.txt` and `weekN_copy.json` (and `league.json`/crest files if changed) to the project folder.

## Writing the copy (voice and rules)

**Less text, more graphics.** The charts carry the story; words are captions. Keep every field near the lengths in `week5_copy.json`.

- **Voice**: tabloid sports desk, dry and specific. The joke lives in the numbers. Roast lineup *decisions* and results, never anyone's looks, family, job or personal life. **No memes** (the user removed them).
- **Every number comes from the data.** No invented quotes, injuries or NFL news. A 0-projection starter is "projected for zero", nothing more.
- **Team tokens** in prose: `{T7}` full name with crest, `{S7}` short name with crest. Headlines and titles are plain text.
- `hero`: `winner`, `loser` (usually the week's lead game), `big` (the headline number, usually the margin), `label` (≤ 6 words). The starter strips under it are automatic.
- `lead`: kicker, headline (≤ 6 words), deck (one sentence, ≤ 20 words), **two** paragraphs of ≤ 35 words.
- `sidebar`: exactly two boxes. Box 1 `style: "hi"` "Strange but true"; box 2 `style: "plain"` analytical angle. Each has a `viz` (`bars` with rows `{team|label, value, alt?}`, or `wins` with `teams`) and ≤ 25 words of text.
- `recaps`: one per game keyed `"winnerId-loserId"`; headline ≤ 6 words, text ≤ 20 words (top performers, the bench verdict). The page-one game's headline is "See Page One".
- `honor`: Manager of the Week = fewest points left on the bench (ties: higher score). `big` with 2 decimals, `big_label` "pts left on bench", text ≤ 30 words.
- `blotter`: 4 teams, title = the charge (≤ 6 words), text ≤ 15 words. Booking number is automatic.
- `missing`: 4 starters with the biggest misses vs projection, names exactly as in facts; text ≤ 8 words.
- `pecking_notes`: all 12 teams, ≤ 6 words each.
- `forecast_blurbs`: one per next-week matchup keyed `"homeId-awayId"`, ≤ 8 words.
- `upsets` (**the week's biggest news**, right below the blue stat tiles, user 2026-10-19): one entry per game in facts' "Upsets & heartbreakers" list, keyed `"winnerId-loserId"`: `headline` ≤ 6 words, `text` ≤ 30 words. **This is the one place to be brutal**: rub salt in the loser's wound (the bench points, the benched player who'd have won it, the record they fell from, the losing streak) and hype the winner like they just won a title. Still roast results and lineup decisions, never people. The first game's headline is the page-one banner (giant type), so make it the best line in the paper. Records, ranks, margin and the red chips are automatic. The blowout is always there, so there is always at least one entry (`data/week5_copy.json` has the example). Another example: `"upsets": {"9-8": {"headline": "Undefeated? Not Anymore", "text": "{S9} walked into the No. 1 team's house at 1–2 and left with the belt. {S8} benched 28 points of Kyle Monangai and lost by 4.16."}}`.
- Automatic sections (no copy): stat tiles, hero strips, Week at a Glance, Boom or Bust, Scoreboard lanes, Points by Position, Position Kings, Season Heat Map, Good or Lucky?, Floor & Ceiling, Bench Hall of Shame, Power Rankings Over Time, Playoff Odds, What If?, Waiver Wire MVP, The Wire (verified transactions), Trade Desk, On the Block, forecast bars.
- **Running lore**: add rivalries/jokes/nicknames the user shares to `league.json` `lore` and weave them in later.

## Trades and the trading block

- **No trade rumors.** The user decided (2026-10-13) to publish only trades that actually happened. ESPN only shows pending/declined/expired offers to the managers involved, so those are never fetched, stored or mentioned.
- **Trade Desk**: completed trades (`TRADE_PROPOSAL` with status EXECUTED) are league-public. The section appears automatically when the season has at least one; otherwise it is omitted.
- **On the Block** lists players managers marked "on the trading block" in ESPN (ON_THE_BLOCK only; untouchable and normal players are never shown), as yellow-tagged cards with week-N points, projection and whether they started. **User's rule (updated 2026-10-13): 10 of 12 teams reporting is enough** (`league.json` `trade_block_min_teams`). The note under the section names teams with no trading block data. If no player is listed, the section shows a "Nobody's selling" card. As of 2026-10-13 ESPN returns the field for 10 of 12 teams (not Kazakhstan [11] or Bedazzled Balls [12]).

## Upsets & heartbreakers (added 2026-10-19 at the user's request; shown untitled under the stat tiles as the week's biggest news)

Computed in `facts.py` (`upsets` in the facts JSON, "Upsets & heartbreakers" in the facts digest), drawn by `render.py`. Records and ranks are **as of kickoff** (standings = wins, then points for, through week N−1).
- **Upset**: the winner had fewer wins going in **and** the loser was a top-`upset_top_n` (4) team going in or had at least `upset_win_gap` (2) more wins. Week 1 never has upsets.
- **Heartbreaker**: margin under `heartbreaker_margin` (2) points.
- **Blowout** (added 2026-10-19): the week's biggest-margin game is always included, so the section appears every week. Its chip adds "Perfect lineup still loses by X" when even the loser's best lineup falls short. A game can carry several stamps.
- Order (first = the full-width banner): upset + heartbreaker, then upsets by win gap, then heartbreakers by margin, then the blowout. In a quiet week the blowout is the banner.
- Fact chips for the loser (no label): points left on the bench; "Best lineup would have won" when true; their best benched player who belonged in the best lineup; a losing streak of 2+.
- The thresholds live in `league.json`; change them only if the user asks.

## What If? notes

- Built by `render.py` from `data/week1_raw.txt` … `data/weekN_raw.txt` using `facts.optimal`. Both teams in every game get their best possible lineup from that week's roster; standings rank by wins, then points for; playoff line from META `playoffteams` (6). Added at the user's request 2026-10-18.
- The section is left out unless every week 1–N has a raw file. If one is missing, dispatch the fetch workflow for that week (`-f 'inputs[week]=K'`); weeks 1–3 were backfilled this way on 2026-10-18. A backfill run rewrites `data/latest.json` to that week; set it back to the latest week afterwards.

## Season sections (added 2026-10-18/19 at the user's request)

All automatic in `render.py`, no copy needed, and all need every `data/weekK_raw.txt` for weeks 1–N (otherwise they're left out; backfill as described under What If?).
- **Power Rankings Over Time**: cumulative all-play wins after each week, tiebreak points for (same rule as the Pecking Order, so the last column matches it).
- **Playoff Odds**: needs `data/schedule.json`. Simulates the remaining regular-season weeks 10,000 times with a fixed seed (20260000 + N, so renders are reproducible). Each team's weekly score ~ Normal(mean, σ): mean = (season points + 3 × league average) / (weeks + 3); σ = league-wide standard deviation of each team's weekly scores around its own average. Standings by wins, then points for; top `playoff_teams` (6) make it. The note under the chart prints the league average and σ.
- **Floor & Ceiling**: min / median / max of each team's weekly scores, plus this week's score.
- **Bench Hall of Shame**: season sum of (best lineup − actual), stacked by week; ✕ marks each loss where the team's best lineup beat the opponent's actual score.
- **Waiver Wire MVP**: every free-agent/waiver ADD in the weekly TX rows; a pickup's points count only in weeks the adding team started him, from the add week on. Top 8 shown. A player re-claimed after being dropped counts as a pickup (e.g. Mahomes to Bedazzled Balls, week 1).

## Tabs and the Player Stats tab (added 2026-10-19 at the user's request)

`template.html` has a tab bar under the masthead, styled as folder tabs (user, 2026-10-19: easier to tell apart): the selected tab is solid royal blue with a yellow base line, the other is outlined on paper; each has a small subtitle ("Week N edition", "PPR player desk"); on phones the two split the width. Tabs: **This Week's Paper** (`#paper`, everything from the stat tiles to the forecast) and **Player Stats** (`#player-stats`, linkable, as is every section inside it). The tab script opens whichever tab holds the linked section.

**Player Stats is automatic (no copy) and follows the edition week** (user, 2026-10-19): the week N edition shows stats and grading through week N and looks ahead to week N+1 (projections, start/sit, rankings). `render.py` passes N; the midweek refresh passes the newest edition's N (`players.py --week N --patch …`), so it never jumps ahead of the paper. Its CSS ships inside the tab (in `players.CSS`, written into the pane), not in `template.html`, so a midweek `--patch` also carries style changes. `template.html` also has a `<meta name="viewport">` (added 2026-10-19) so the league site uses the phone layout on phones. `render.py` fills `<!--PLAYERSTATS-->` with `players.build()` from `data/players/*.json`; the pane sits between `<!--PS-->` and `<!--/PS-->` so it can be swapped in place. Sections, all PPR:
1. **Current Data**: usage board by position (snap %, target share, targets/g, touches/g, air-yards share, PPR/g, last-3 trend, league owner or FA).
2. **Volume vs. Scoring**: the regression watch, interactive (user, 2026-10-19): a player search box, RB/WR/TE and free-agent filters, and a player card on hover or tap (owner, PPR/g vs. expected, targets, carries, target share, snap %); hover and taps snap to the nearest dot, and on phones the card docks at the bottom of the screen. Clicking a Running hot/cold name highlights him on the chart. Every RB/WR/TE with 3+ games and 4+ expected PPR/g is plotted (~210); the hot/cold lists still come from the 70 biggest-volume players. Expected PPR/g = least-squares fit, per position, of PPR/g on targets/g, carries/g and air yards/g (QBs: pass attempts/g and carries/g), players with 3+ games. Scatter of expected vs. actual plus "Running hot" (sell-high) and "Running cold" (buy-low) lists.
3. **Week N+1 Projections** (N = edition week): blended projection = mean of FantasyPros consensus projected points and ESPN's projection; the bar shows the range between them.
4. **Start / Sit Assist**: pick any two players (grouped by league team, then free agents). Every rostered player is listed, including bye-week and ruled-out players (labelled "no game" / "out" and always sat), plus every free agent projected for 1+ point (user, 2026-10-19). Score = projection + 0.35 × floor + recent target share and snaps, with injury penalties. Defaults to the closest FLEX call on the owner's team (league.json `owner_team`). "Contrarian lean" = Joel Smyth ranks the player well above/below consensus that week.
5. **Week N+1 Rankings**: FantasyPros consensus (ECR) with rank range, start/sit grade, projection; free agents in our league highlighted yellow. Falls back to projection order until the consensus is posted.
6. **Rest-of-Season Rankings**: FantasyPros ROS PPR consensus with weekly move, PPR/g and expected PPR/g, and an "On our wire" strip of the best-ranked free agents.
7. **Rankings vs. Actuals**: every completed week's consensus ranks (top 60 WR, 48 RB, 24 QB, 24 TE; deepened 2026-10-19 at the user's request) graded against actual positional finishes; average rank miss per position, projection miss in points, last week's 10 biggest beats and misses, the season's 12 most underrated/overrated players, and a searchable "Every graded player" table by position.

**Sources stay in the background** (user, 2026-10-19): the page shows blended numbers and never a "who said what" panel. The only source line is the small footnote at the bottom of the tab.

**Data** (`fetch_players.py`, GitHub Actions; this workspace can't reach these sites directly):
- ESPN league API (same cookies): weekly box scores and PPR points for the 600 most-rostered QB/RB/WR/TE, ESPN projections for the current and next week, league owner, injury status. ESPN stat ids were verified by rebuilding every week-5 PPR score exactly.
- nflverse (GitHub releases, public): weekly target share, air-yards share, WOPR; PFR snap counts matched by normalized name (98% of player-weeks).
- FantasyPros public pages (`var ecrData` JSON): weekly PPR consensus for every week (past weeks saved once in `data/players/fp_weekK.json`, the live and next week refreshed every run), ROS PPR consensus, and the public "Joel Smyth vs. Expert Consensus" disagreements.
- **Gaps**: Luke Garza is not used (paid Substack, user doesn't subscribe). Joel Smyth's full list loads client-side on Yahoo and his full FantasyPros list needs a signed-in account, so only his notable disagreements are used. FantasyPros' projection tables show 10 rows without sign-in; the consensus projected points in the ranking data cover every ranked player instead. The user's own regression model will slot in later (user, 2026-10-19: "skip my model for now").

**Schedule**: Tuesday's ESPN fetch (`fetch.yml`) also runs `fetch_players.py`, so the 6am edition carries fresh player data. `players.yml` refreshes the tab on the newest edition (docs/index.html, docs/weekN.html, daily-fantasizer-weekN.html) Tue/Wed/Thu/Sat 11:17am and Sun 11:47am Eastern as next week's consensus fills in, then asks GitHub Pages to rebuild.

## Known quirks

- ESPN `record.overall` ignores in-progress games; facts.py computes records from the schedule.
- `TRADE_ACCEPT` rows carry no players; executed trades come from `TRADE_PROPOSAL` rows with status EXECUTED.
- Waiver adds not on the week's roster are marked UNVERIFIED by facts.py and left out of The Wire.
- Playoff weeks: check `regweeks` in META and the schedule's matchup periods when week 15 arrives. If `next_week` is empty the forecast disappears automatically.
